# The world: the lot, people on foot, and the routes round it

Christian's first play test (2026-10-11): "Players should be able to spawn into the lot which shows all the trucks … go up to a person or hit a key to buy the vehicles. This allows players to socialize in the lot and look in the distance to see other players." Until then the lot was a menu and a player was their truck from the first frame.

## The lot is a place, south of every route

`DdLot` is a slab with a fence round it, laid out in its own frame with -Z toward the mountain. From south to north: the spawn line, the showroom (a truck of every model in the catalogue on a plinth, its name and price over it, and a salesperson in the lot's amber beside it), the parking bays, and the north fence the roads leave through. `DdGame._build_lot` puts it under the middle of the routes' starts and `LOT_CLEARANCE` (170 m) south of the furthest south any road reaches, so the sea and the islands' slopes lie between; it is rebuilt with the routes, so another mountain moves it, and everybody on foot is put back on its spawn.

A server builds it too, undrawn: the slab and the fence are floors and walls, and the spots (spawn, bays, sellers) are where the world puts people and trucks and what it checks a use against. The showroom's trucks, people, signs and lamps are drawn only; each display truck is a frozen box so nobody walks through it.

## People walk on their own machine, and the world only refuses

A person joins on foot at the next spawn (`DdGame.join`, `_put_on_foot`); stand-ins never walk. On the player's machine `DdWalker` is a `CharacterBody3D` driven by dot-player-controller's third-person motor (`DotTpsController`, its camera off) and drawn as one of the family's people (`DotHumanoidFigure`, dot-player-char). The driving rows walk (W ahead, S back, A and D sideways), the handbrake's key jumps and Shift runs; the mouse looks round, so the pointer is captured on foot.

**Walking is the owner's, not predicted against the server.** This game predicts nothing (a truck is a rigid body, dot-vehicle's decision), so there is no rewind to keep a walker in step with, and a round trip on every step is the one latency a player feels at once. Nothing on foot can hurt anybody or win anything. So the owner moves, and says where they got to: offline straight to `DdGame.walk`, connected after the drive in every input packet (`DdEvents.write_walk`: a bit, three floats and ten bits of facing while on foot, one bit while driving). `walk` refuses a step longer than a run allows in one report (`WALK_STEP_MAX`, room for a few lost packets) and a place nobody on foot may be (`walkable`), and answers with where to stand (`walker_placed`, a PLACE to the owner), at most twice a second. Below `WALK_FLOOR` a walker is put back on the lot.

What everybody else sees of somebody on foot is `Driver.walker`, a plain node the world moves when it takes a step. The server replicates it (`DdWalkerNet`: position and a ten-bit facing, interpolated); each client hangs a person and a name tag under every walker but its own (`DdClient._present_walkers`), and the person animates from how far the node moved (`auto_animate`), so nothing about gait is sent. DRIVER carries `on_foot` and the walker's net id; the owner's own mirror is never drawn, because its body is.

## The camera on foot

One camera for walking, driving and the lot, so the settings' field of view and the rock's shake reach all three. On foot it sits behind and over the eyes, swept from the head by its own `DotCameraProbe` against the mountain's layer and layer 1 (a truck, a display or the fence is something to look past); F5 toggles first person where `mp_forceview` is 0, and 1 is first person only.

## Buying, and a truck off the road

A salesperson stands beside every display. The use key (E) is one ACT, `use`, and what it does is `DdGame.use_choice`: beside your parked truck, get in; beside a salesperson, buy their truck if you can pay for it, or have it brought round if it is yours; at the wheel off a road and nearly stopped (`GET_OUT_SPEED`), get out. The HUD's line for E is the same function asked on the client, with the GARAGE's money and trucks on a mirror and the walker's own position, so the words and the server's choice cannot differ. The lot's panel (G) is the other way to buy, anywhere in the lot; its trucks column buys one you do not own and DRIVES one you do. Buying, choosing and upgrading are refused anywhere but the lot (`in_lot`, checked in `DdBridgeActs`); stand-ins and suites call the world directly.

A driver has one truck. Off a road it is empty (no load, no trailer): `drive_off` builds it in the free bay nearest the display (`DdLot.free_bay`, never on top of somebody else's) with them at the wheel; `get_out` leaves it parked on its brakes and puts them on foot beside the driver's door (`door_of`); `get_in` from beside it. `_step_roaming` drives it with their command or holds it, and puts one that fell off the world or lies on its side back in a bay. A trip replaces it with the loaded truck (and trailer); back from the road (`end_trip`, Escape) it is parked empty in a bay again with them on foot beside it, or they are on the spawn when every bay is full. Another mountain takes every truck away and puts everybody on the new lot's spawn.

On the wire a truck made for anybody, trip or not, is `truck_made`: the bridge forgets the bodies that are no longer theirs and replicates the new ones; getting in and out is `seated`, a DRIVER (with `on_foot`). Off a road a client draws its own truck with the chase camera and the HUD's speed, no trip.

## The land, a road to every route, and a gate on each

`DdLand` is one heightfield round the lot, built from the routes, the lot and the roads alone, so every machine builds the same land from what HELLO already carries and nothing about it is sent. It is the sea within `SEA_NEAR` of any route's road (an island keeps its own mountain, and nobody on foot reaches it but by a road), plain beyond `SEA_FAR`, with hills growing away from the roads and the lot, and a coast a wandering `COAST` out from them (distances by chamfer sweeps, noise from `DdWeather.unit`). The lot and every road are flat `UNDER` (0.4 m) below their deck or slab, a bank down and a cut up beyond; 5 cm striped the lot from 300 m off. **It collides** (a `HeightMapShape3D`, uniformly scaled by a cell, on layer 1 and the camera's), unlike an island's mountain: nothing on it is a route, so a route's road is still its only floor.

A road leaves the lot's north fence for every route, west to east so none cross (`routes_west_to_east`, `_road_line`: straight out, a cubic bend, straight up to the gate, on under the barrier to the route's pad). Its deck is its own trimesh at the lot's height with a bevelled edge down to the ground, so a walker walks onto it rather than meeting a kerb; the land banks it over the water. A road's bank has no round end at the route's start: past it are the route's pad and the sea, and a bank there was a slope round the gate.

Each island's mountain waits for the roads (`DdRoute.terrain_later`, `finish_terrain`) and is held under every road sample on its ground, with nothing growing on them; `headless_run` checks no mountain stands over a road.

`DdGate` stands where a road meets its route, `GATE_SETBACK` past the start pad. **A delivery is accepted at the gate, not past it**: the barrier (a low wall under a striped boom, on layer 1) stops any truck; at it (`at_gate`) E offers the route's load, or says the route is still locked; accepted (`DdGame.accept`), the route's own loaded truck and trailer are put down on the start line past the barrier, which is how a trip always began, so the barrier never opens and nothing follows anybody through. Either side of the road a wall on `DdLot.WALKER_WALL` stops people only, and the world refuses a walker past the line (`beyond`, in `walkable`). The lot's SET OFF drives the player's truck to the gate (`go_to_gate`); `quick_start` (config, off) makes it the trip itself, as before the lot had roads, which suites and tools use.

## A map is a document

`DdMapDoc` is what a map is: which routes the world holds and how it is laid out (`layout.gap`, a route's `place`, the `lot`, its `clearance`, a road's `roads` points, the `land`'s hills and coast). Everything but the id is optional; a map that says nothing about its layout gets the layout every map had before maps had one, so the whole range, Low Country and High Country are three short files. The game's own are `maps/*.json` (`DdMountains.shipped`, read once, in `order`); a server's map packs keep theirs in `routes/` beside the routes they need, and `DdGame.load_directory` sorts each document by its `kind`. A world's catalogue is `DdGame.maps`; the vote and the M list are `DdMountains.allowed(library, maps)`, which offers two maps of the same routes only when their layouts differ (The Long Haul is the whole range laid out far apart).

**The client is sent the map with the routes** (HELLO's and MOUNTAIN's `map`, through `adopt_documents`), so a map only the server has is built the same on every client, and nothing about the land, the lot or the gates is sent beyond it: they are computed from the documents. A placement is a place, never a turn: every route still starts facing north, and its geometry and queries are in its own frame offset by its position. A road through a map's points is a Catmull-Rom curve between the lot's straight start and the gate's straight approach; `headless_run` builds every shipped map and fails one whose roads cross, miss a gate, or reach past what the netcode places.

## Streaming, draw distance and the browser

Nothing is streamed: the world is built whole on a join and on a mountain change, because everything in it is computed from documents a client already has, and the costliest part is the islands' mountains it always built. What the land adds is one mesh and one heightfield shape, capped at `MAX_ACROSS` samples across (a wider map gets coarser land, The Long Haul 374 by 153 at 8 m), built in a few hundred milliseconds on a server. What is drawn far off is kept cheap: the display trucks' signs stop at 260 m, a salesperson's tag at 70 m and a walker's name at 90 m, the people themselves are dot-player-char's (their own animation and draw ranges), the land and the mountains cast no shadow, and the fog the weather already sets hides the far edge. The join is unchanged in shape (the map document is a few hundred bytes beside the routes), so the join-burst budget is the routes', measured by dot-server-deploy's `tools/burst_all_games.sh`.
