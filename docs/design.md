# The design

A person arrives on foot in the lot and walks wherever the land goes ([world.md](world.md)); **at the wheel a player is their truck**, from the bay or the gate to the depot. So dot-vehicle's seat handover (`DotVehicleRide`, the spawner) is not used: `DdTruck` binds a `DotVehicleInstance` and a `DotVehicleWheeled` chassis itself, and `DdGame` calls `drive()` once a tick, the same call the spawner makes.

## One world holds every route, and a route is a level

The brief asked for a big map whose corners have their own weather, and for levels that get harder. Both are this: `DdGame.build_routes` lays every route document out as the map says (side by side along +X, its `layout.gap` apart, unless the map places one: [world.md](world.md#a-map-is-a-document)), each route is a level by its document's `level`, and each has its own zones. A player takes a route at its gate, at the end of the land's road to it. A server owner's new route is a file in `routes/` (written by `tools/build_routes.py`) and a new corner of the map. `levels_unlock_in_order` opens level N once any route of level N-1 is delivered (or the nearest level below if one is missing).

**Hairpins put the cliff on the inside**, always (`build_routes.py`'s `hairpin()`): every truck cuts the corner, and with the drop inside a stand-in fell 41 times in one bend. **And a rail round the outside, on a road 1.5 m wider than its own** (`HAIRPIN_WIDEN`), as real switchbacks are: the first play test rolled off the outside of a snowy hairpin at walking pace with nothing there. The danger of a hairpin is the speed brought into it, not a lottery at 5 km/h. A warning board stands 45 m before every hairpin and chevrons line its rail (`DdRoute._build_signs`); stand-ins swing wide round one (`route_points`), as a long truck must.

A change of width eases over 10 m (`DdRoute.widths`, `WIDTH_EASE`) rather than stepping at the segment boundary, where a cliff's edge made a corner of rock square to the road.

**Hold the left button and drag to orbit the chase or high camera** (`_orbit`, `_unhandled_input`): the same scale and signs as the on-foot look (`MOUSE_SENSITIVITY`, yaw and pitch as `DotTpsMotor` takes them), added to the camera's wanted place round `PIVOT_HEIGHT` before it is eased and swept, so the orbited camera is still kept out of the rock by the same probe. Let go and it eases back behind the truck. The cab view keeps looking out of the windscreen, and the pointer mode is untouched (`pointer_mode`).

**The driver is drawn in the cab.** `DdClient._seat_driver` hangs a `DotHumanoidFigure` (the same person as the walker, `look_of`) under every truck, seated with its hands on the wheel (the SIT and DRIVE clips) at `DdTrucks.SEATS`: a seat point and a height per model, because the Kenney cabs differ and a full-size person does not fit behind the glass of the smaller ones. It is a child of the truck, so it rides the truck's snapshot with nothing of its own to sync; it has no body and is on no physics layer, so it neither collides nor stops the camera. It is hidden while the player is on foot (the walker is the person then) and, for the local player, in the cab view. dot-vehicle's `DotVehicleRide` is not used: it reparents the figure to a seat, and a person who is also a walker would have to be handed back on every exit and every snapshot.

**The body no longer rocks on a straight road (2026-10-16).** At 20 to 45 km/h the body rolled 2 to 5 degrees every two to three seconds and the flatbed slid: the server is level (nothing but the wind pushes it, `truck.push`, 2.6 m/s² times the gust times `wind_scale`), and the client mirror reproduces the server's roll to within a tenth of a degree (4.11 against 4.13), so it was not the snapshots (their quantisation steps are about 0.15 degrees), it was the suspension at stiffness 60 swinging on a side force. `DdTrucks.tunables_for` now uses stiffness 75 (was 60) with compression damping 0.9 and relaxation 1.25 (was 0.85 and 0.95). Stiffer is better for roll (110 halved it and ended the flatbed's slide) but `headless_run` caps it: at 110 the Semi's trailer test crawled, and a truck put down hopped off two wheels for a few ticks when the settle hold let go, which more relaxation damping removes at 75 but at 1.5 slows the Semi. `top_speed` and `linear_damp` are untouched and every suite passes.

**A stand-in turned a truck over on the Slate Quarry (2026-10-18), and the cab's glass is see-through now.** `tools/drive.sh` now prints the worst tilt and every flip (`TILT`, `FLIP`): every route is under 13 degrees except the Slate Quarry, where the stand-in drove into the pile of debris at 553 m at 17 m/s and rolled 10 times over 600 s. The cause was `DdRoute.route_points`: the pile's dodge (0.26 of the width into the other lane) and the oncoming car's keep-right (0.24 the other way) cancelled where a car's stretch overlaps a pile's. A dodge now wins over keep-right (the car stops for a truck in its lane anyway). `handling`'s `piles` section checks the stand-in's route is in the other lane from every pile and drives that pile at 17 m/s (fails at tilt 81 degrees with the old code); the hairpin section checks no truck leans past 30 degrees. The suspension itself was never the roll: the box truck's worst lean on any route is the road's own grade. **The glass:** the Kenney cabs paint their windows on the body's one surface (colormap texels with b = 255 and g - r >= 10); `DdTruck._open_glass` splits those triangles onto a translucent surface at load (the CC0 .glb files stay as shipped), so the seated driver (`DdTrucks.SEATS`, now inside the cab) is seen through the windows from the side and, on an open-bed truck, from behind. A box truck's cargo box still hides the driver from behind, as a real one does.

## How fast a truck goes is the truck and the road, never a ceiling

The first play test read 68 to 69 km/h on every straight of every route: the stock chassis pushed with its whole `engine_force` up to `top_speed` (19 m/s, the Box Truck's number) and nothing above it, and the body's default damping (0.1 a second, proportional to speed) was the only thing pushing back. `DdDrivetrain` (a `DotVehicleWheeled` subclass by path, dot-vehicle's extension point) replaces the force a throttle is worth:

- **An engine through a gearbox**: `power_kw` and `torque_nm` (most of it from idle, all from 1100 rpm, then whatever the power gives, nothing past `redline_rpm`) through `gears` and `final_drive` on wheels of the model's size. The box is automatic: up at a shift point that rises with the throttle, down below its own (never twice in 0.8 s, so it never hunts), and nothing drives the wheels for the 0.35 s a shift takes, which is what makes a heavy truck lose speed at every change on a climb. A truck put down anywhere is back in first.
- **Against the air, the tyres and the slope**: `drag_area` times the speed squared and `rolling` times the weight, applied by the drivetrain; the slope is the physics engine's own gravity. So the top speed is where they meet: the governor (`governor_kmh`, a real truck has one) on the flat for a light load, far below it up a climb with a full one. The truck body's linear damping is off (`DAMP_MODE_REPLACE`, 0): at the project default it was 8.5 kN of drag at 19 m/s on the Box Truck, more than the air.
- **Traction**: the pull is capped at 0.42 g of the truck's weight plus a quarter of a trailer's (`hitch_mass`). First gear's torque through the gearing was 86 kN on the Bulk Carrier, which stood it on its tail on a climb.
- **Brakes are newtons.** Godot's raycast vehicle reads a wheel's `brake` as an impulse per physics step (Bullet's `maxImpulse`), not a force: the stock chassis's `brake_force / wheels` stopped a loaded Flatbed from 50 km/h in 0.7 m. dot-vehicle's chassis multiplies by the step (4f4afaf; the drivetrain did it here first). The body's damping is `tunables.linear_damp`, 0 for every truck, because dot-vehicle writes it onto the body at bind. A trailer brakes itself at 5 m/s² for its mass with the tractor (`DdTrailer.set_braking`).
- The HUD shows the gear beside the speed; the trip view sends it to a connected client.

`examples/handling` measures every truck and every load (headless numbers, printed as tables) and checks that they differ. At the time of writing, with crates: 0-50 km/h from 6.0 s (Flatbed) to 10.4 s (Bulk Carrier), flat top speed 73 to 97 km/h (governors), an 8% climb 50 to 72 km/h, a stop from 50 km/h in 17 to 28 m.

## A load is a share of the truck, and it changes the drive

`DdCargo` is the loads as data, and a route names its load (`cargo` in the document: crates, timber, fuel, glass, explosives; gravel is there for an owner's routes). A load is a fraction of the truck's `capacity_kg` (`fill`), so the same timber is a heavier haul in a bigger truck. It adds its mass (on the trailer for a truck that pulls one), raises the centre of mass (`com_raise`), catches the wind (`wind`), multiplies the pay (`pay`) and the knocks (`fragile`: the knock threshold divided, the loss multiplied). Fuel `slosh`es: a share of its mass is a spring-damped mass in the truck's plane (`DdTruck._slosh_step`, 0.55 Hz, lightly damped), which pushes the truck on after a turn or a stop and rolls it, a beat late; steady in a long bend it is the same as a solid load. Measured: the Hauler climbs 8% at 51 km/h with gravel and 78 with glass; after a swerve at 50 km/h a tanker keeps yawing five times as far as timber does.

The Semi's trailer carries its load ahead of its rear axles (a quarter on the hitch) and hitches over the tractor's rear axle, as a fifth wheel does, with a drawbar back to the box. Hung out behind the tractor's tail (where the empty 2.6 t trailer used to hitch), a 9 t load was a lever that lifted the tractor's front wheels on a climb.

## Every route has challenges, a few hundred metres apart at most

The first play test found long corridors between a cliff and a drop with nothing on the road. A route document now places, per segment: falling rock (`boulders`), black ice (`ice`), a pile in one lane (`debris`), **roadworks** (`works`: barriers closing 42% of the road for 27 m, coned in; the validator refuses a road that leaves less than 3.3 m open), **loose gravel** (`gravel`: 40 m of lower grip, `gravel_grip`, and a washboard every 0.22 s whose jolt grows with speed, `gravel_jolt`, which the knock test charges to the load: glass across it at 70 km/h loses a quarter, at 25 km/h nothing) and **oncoming traffic** (`traffic`). **Narrow ledges** (`ledge`: dot-props' `DotPropLedge`, a tapered rock outcrop growing out of the cliff's side for 24 m and leaving 56% of the road, drop beside it; a mountain's `hazards.ledge` adds them, High Country has two per route). With narrowings (a segment narrower than the route), hairpins and the weather zones, `DdRoute.challenges()` lists them; the lot shows the count and `handling` checks that every shipped route has five kinds or more, traffic among them, and never 240 m without one.

**Oncoming traffic is a Kenney car down the other lane, on the tick's schedule.** Each run starts when `(now + phase × cycle) / cycle` turns over, so every machine starts it at the same moment and a machine that joins mid-run puts the car where the arithmetic says; nothing is sent. Within a run the car stops for a truck in its lane within 14 m (`_car_blocked`), from the truck and trailer positions the world has, which a client has too: a kinematic car stops for nothing by itself, and the first version shoved a truck straddling the line thirty metres back down a snowy road at 11 m/s. The car hugs its own edge, runs 20 m clear of each end of its segment (where checkpoints put trucks), and only on segments 80 m or longer, 6.4 m wide and nearly straight (the validator). Stand-ins keep right where a car runs.

## Put down, a truck is held, and one hung up is put back

A truck put on a checkpoint (a fall, R, a skip) is held on its brakes for `SETTLE_SECONDS` (0.4) before it is the driver's: driven at once on a steep snowy grade, a loaded Semi rolled back before its tyres had settled and went over. **A truck hung up** (two or more wheels off the road and slower than 1 m/s for `hung_respawn_seconds`, 3.5) is put back like a fall: the first play test sat at 0 to 1 km/h on the River Cut's open edge with two wheels over it until R. `handling` drives the Semi and the Bulk Carrier off every start line and checkpoint of every route, and a careful driver (a stand-in at 7 m/s) round the eight hairpins of Snowline Pass, Wind Ridge, Devil's Spine and Slate Quarry on snow in three trucks.

## A mountain is a set of routes, and the players vote for the next one

**A mountain also shapes its routes** (`shape` in the map document, `DdRouteDoc.reshape`, applied before the hazard overlay in `DdGame._select_mountain`, so every machine agrees): `mirror` turns every bend and its cliff and rail the other way, `stretch` lengthens the straights (0.6 to 2, climb with them), `twist` scales the bends (0.5 to 1.4), `climb` the grade, `narrow` takes metres off the width (never below 5.6 m). Hairpins and checkpoints are kept, so a stage count never changes. Low Country is shorter, straighter and flatter; High Country is mirrored, longer, twistier, steeper; The Long Haul stretches every road 1.6 times. The same route is therefore a different length and shape per mountain (`headless_run` checks Snowline Pass has four lengths), on top of each mountain's hazards and weather.

Every route is already open to everybody, so a ballot over single routes decides nothing. What a server can change for everybody at once is which routes the world holds: `DdMountains` names three (`range`, every route loaded and the default; `low_country`, levels 1 to 3; `high_country`, levels 3 to 5), and `DdGame.set_mountain` rebuilds the world on one from `library` (every document accepted) into `documents` (the mountain's). A smaller mountain puts the same drivers on three or four roads instead of six (Slate Quarry, the second level-2 route, joined Low Country on 2026-10-16), sharing the road, the weather and the rock. A delivered route pack joins the whole range only.

- **No rounds, so the ballot runs on dot-vote's own clock** (`DdVote`, `trigger: time_limit`): a mountain lasts `mountain_minutes` (15, the `dd_vote_minutes` cvar), the ballot opens a minute before, and its winner is applied when the time is up. `!rtv` opens one early and applies it at once.
- **The hauls on the road are the "round" the change waits for** (`time_up: finish_round`, at most 180 s): a change ends every trip, so `DdModule._game_tick` reports the road clear (`note_road_clear`) only when no person is mid-haul. Stand-ins never hold it up. The same answer is dot-vote's `round_in_progress_fn` (`_anyone_hauling`, set by name because an older shell's dot-vote lacks it), so an empty road lets the change through at once; the tick's `note_road_clear` stays for that older shell.
- **A mountain's lowest level is always open** (`is_unlocked` counts over the routes the world holds), so High Country is drivable by somebody who has delivered nothing.
- **Every trip ends and the rock goes** before the rebuild: the road a truck was on may not exist any more. The old route nodes leave the tree before the new ones arrive (same names), and the weather cache is cleared so every zone is painted again.
- **The ballot is the shell's**: the `map_ballot` notice drawn by `DotBallotPanel`, as in the other games; `tools/shot.sh --ballot` renders it. The server-wide game vote is dot-server-deploy's and separate (its commands are `game_`-prefixed); this vote's director stays out of the registry (`register_service` off) and waits for a votekick (`busy_fn`).

## Weather is the world's, and a trip is paid for what it met

Several trucks share a mountain, so the sky over a zone is `DdWeather.at(seed, "route|zone", zone, tick)`, a hash per `PERIOD_SECONDS` (150) window, the same for everybody on it.

- **Never Godot's `hash()` for a roll.** It is djb2: strings differing in their last character differ only in low bits, so every period of a zone rolled 0.2816 to 0.2819 and a 50% zone was snowy always or never. `DdWeather.unit()` takes MD5's first 32 bits. Boulders, hazards and the terrain's noise use it too.
- **Chaos is counted from what a trip met** (`DdTrip.meet`: each zone's sky and wind once, each rock site once): a bonus for a blizzard nobody drove through is a bonus for nothing.
- Snow paints a zone's road white (`DdRoute.paint_zone`) on every machine from the same function. `DdGame.force_weather(sky, wind)` overrides every zone, including road no zone names: an operator's tool and the screenshot tool's.
- **Black ice and debris are part of the road, not the weather.** A segment's `ice` and `debris` counts are laid by `DdRoute._place_hazards` from `DdWeather.unit()` of route, segment and index, so every machine lays the same. Ice drops grip to `ice_grip` and is paid once as `ice_chaos`. A debris pile takes one lane, never both (a pile across the road is a road nobody can drive); `route_points` swings a stand-in round it.

**Extreme weather is two more rolls of the same draw.** A storm is a rain period that came up again against `storm_chance`: always wet and windy, gusts `storm_gust` times as hard, grip times `storm_grip`, paid `storm_chaos`. Fog is a snow-free period that came up against `fog_chance`: the client thickens the fog to `fog_density` and the HUD says so, paid `fog_chaos`. Both scale with `weather_frequency` and come from `DdWeather.at` alone, so a client agrees from the seed. Cvars `dd_weather_frequency`, `dd_weather_storms`, `dd_weather_fog` and `dd_weather_disasters` change the numbers and re-send them (`DdGame.weather_config`); `dd_weather storm|fog|clear|rain|snow|wind|off` forces one. 0 on any of them switches that weather off.

**natural-disasters is optional.** `game/dd_disasters.gd` loads its manager by path (the addon is a gitignored link, `addons/natural_disasters`); when it is there, a storm period starts one thunderstorm over its zone (bolts, thunder, shake; a bolt on a truck spoils some load), the server sends the addon's messages as `DdEvents.Kind.DISASTER`, and a joiner is sent `send_snapshot` after HELLO. Absent, nothing is registered and the suites pass with fewer checks. `handling` turns storms and fog off for its 40 m start-line test; `headless_run` and `headless_net` check them.

**Bridges, wrecks and spray.** A bridge deck sags under a heavy truck and comes down past its rating (dot-props), and the server replicates each step as BRIDGE ([netcode](netcode.md)). A truck that goes off a road is a wreck that lands on the mountain, or on a shelf under the road on a server. On a wet road the driver's own tyres throw mist (dot-vehicle's `DotVehicleSpray`). `tools/shot.sh --bridge=collapse` renders a deck coming down.

**Stage hazards are the addon's tornado, wind gusts and quake, set off by the road.** `cfg/game/mg-dangerous-delivery/natural_disasters/stage_hazards.yml` (read by `DotAddonConfig`, so an operator's `cfg/server/game/mg-dangerous-delivery/natural_disasters/` file overrides it, and `dd_stage_hazards 0` switches them all off) lists the kinds each route brings; a route not listed has none. Sites are a pure function of the seed (`DdDisasters.site_distances`, never within `start_margin_m` or `finish_margin_m` of the ends). A driving truck coming within `lead_metres` of a site sets it off: `DdDisasters` sends `{"t": "dd_warn"}` on the same DISASTER message (the client's `warned` signal: siren `DdAudio.HAZARD_WARNING` and the HUD's "TORNADO AHEAD (6 s)"), and `warning_seconds` later starts the addon's own kind at the settings in the yml, which the server simulates and the clients draw. The numbers are small on purpose (a push under half a g averaged over a quake's 0.15 s pulse period, a kick under 1 m/s, a wind gust that costs a truck under 3 m/s, a quake over in 8 s) and `headless_run` checks them; the addon's `wind_at` is counted only for the thunderstorm, because the other kinds push the trucks themselves. Hazards do not change pay. `tools/shot.sh --view=drive --route=dd_wind_ridge --at=300 --sky=clear --hazard=tornado` renders one.

## Falls are a height test, and rock is rate-limited

- **Off the mountain is a height test**: `truck.y < road height at the nearest sample - fall_depth`, not a trigger volume (something falling fast steps over a volume). Flipped and still for `flipped_respawn_seconds` is a fall too.
- A fall costs `fall_cargo_loss` and puts the truck on its last checkpoint after `respawn_seconds`. **R (respawn) costs the same**, or a player about to go over would press it for free.
- **A truck put down is a ghost** to other trucks (`ghost_seconds`, and until it is clear of every other truck): two stand-ins put on one start line jammed at full throttle when their ghost time ran out while they still overlapped.
- **Rock** comes down when a driving truck is `boulder_trigger_distance` short of a site, on `boulder_chance`, then not again for `boulder_cooldown_seconds` (60): at 20 s a slow truck sent back to the start was met by the same rock every time and fell eleven times in a row. Rock starts above the cliff top (at 9 m it started behind a 16 m face and the cliff caught it).

## Solo is collision exceptions and visibility

`DdGame._refresh_exceptions` makes every pair of trucks collide unless either is solo, either is a ghost, or `trucks_collide` is off, both ways, recomputed whenever a truck appears, goes or changes. `DdGame.sees(viewer, other)`: a solo viewer sees nobody; a solo truck is hidden from others unless `solo_hidden_from_others` is off (a truck others can see and drive through is a ghost, which is worse than one that is not there). Chat is not affected. Another truck's positional engine is silent when that truck is hidden.

## The Semi pulls a trailer, on a joint

Any truck with `"trailer": {length, mass}` in `DdTrucks` pulls a `DdTrailer`: a `VehicleBody3D` on four free-rolling wheels (a box dragged along the road is friction that stops a truck on a climb), hitched by a `Generic6DOFJoint3D` that swings ±80°, nods ±18° and rolls hardly at all (a trailer that could roll on its own would leave the road while the truck stayed on it). It takes the road's grip, shares its truck's collision exceptions (solo, ghost), and is replicated as its own body. **Every teleport re-hitches it** (`DdTrailer.rehitch`): a joint whose two bodies jumped apart yanks them back together, dragging the truck back to its trailer and spilling the load. `trailers` (on) turns them all off.

## Trucks, money, the bank

`DdTrucks` is the catalogue and the upgrades as data; `tunables_for()` is what a truck drives with. `DdTruck` builds a `VehicleBody3D` from a Kenney model: hull from its bounds, wheels from its wheel nodes. Harder trucks and worse weather pay more (`DdTrip`'s pay formula). Money is keyed by the session's account uid (`uid:…`), which dot-server has at connect; the platform's profile arrives after seating, too late to key by. The bank's store is `DdModule.bank_file` (JSON) unless a host sets `DdModule.bank_driver` to a dot-moderation-shaped SQL driver.

## The lot makes room for a ballot

The client shell draws every ballot (the server's game vote and this game's mountain vote) as a `DotBallotPanel` in a column down the right-hand side, over whatever the game drew, and the lot's third column (upgrades) read through the translucent panel. The shell cannot reach into a delivered game, so the lot looks: `DdGarage.make_room()` runs every frame the lot is up, reads every visible Control in dot-ui's `dot_hud_cover` group (by that string, so the pack still loads on a shell whose dot-ui predates the group), and when one overlaps the lot the lot eases left and narrows to end 16 px short of it (16..888 at 1280 wide), then eases back when the panel goes. Every button wraps, so the three columns reflow rather than push the lot back under the panel.

- **Not a column cut**: the upgrades are a third of what the lot is for, and a player waiting out a vote is a player at the lot.
- **Not shrunk in place**: a centred lot narrow enough to clear the column wastes the left margin for nothing.
- **Narrower than 720 px it stays centred**, under the panel: three columns a word wide are worse. That is a phone in landscape, where the column is a third of the frame.
- `tools/shot.sh --ballot` renders it; `headless_run` checks where the lot goes.

**And for the chat log under it.** The chat box is dot-game's, bottom-left on a layer above the lot; a full log (eight rows, 518 to 678 px at 720) lay over the routes column from its third row up, so a server vote's lines arriving while a driver chose a route were printed through the route list. While the log draws rows, the lot rises (to 16 px from the top) and then shortens to end 16 px above them, never below `LOT_MIN_HEIGHT` (400); its columns scroll, so a shorter lot loses rows on screen and nothing else. Only the rows being drawn count, so an empty log costs nothing, and the lot eases back as lines expire. Not the log hidden behind the lot: the lot is where a driver waits out a vote, and its lines are what they wait for. `tools/shot.sh --ballot --chat=8` renders it.

## Settings, behind a button

`DdSettings` is dot-menu's `DotMenuSettings` with this game's schema: field of view (SERVER_CLAMPED, default 70), master and effects volume (bound to `DdAudio`'s mixer) and shake (bound to the client's `DotFxManager`). The stock keys nothing here reads are listed in `NOT_APPLIED` (there is no sensitivity: the mouse turns nothing). **The lot's SETTINGS button and O open it, never Escape**, because Escape is how a driver gets back to the lot. The lot hides while the screen is up, because the panel is translucent and the route list printed through it.

## Sound is a dot-audio catalogue, and every loop is shaped

`DdAudio` is the family's shape: ids (`engine`, `engine_other`, `rain`, `wind`, `brake_hiss`, `rockfall`, `delivered`) in a `DotAudioCatalogue` naming `audio/<id>.ogg`, all on the SFX bus, played through a `DotAudioManager`. None of dot-audio's stock voices is a diesel, rain or wind, so `stand_ins()` bakes this game's own into the sink's bank (the diesel's 50 Hz fundamental with odd harmonics and a 25 Hz firing pulse, filtered-noise rain and wind, a noise hiss, a low rumble, a two-note chime); a real `.ogg` at the path wins, one id at a time.

**The levels are named constants in `DdAudio`** (`OWN_IDLE_DB`, `OWN_FULL_DB`, `OTHER_DB`, `RAIN_DB`, `WIND_DB`, `SHOT_DB`) and measured with `tools/loudness.sh` (the Master bus recorded under xvfb, through ffmpeg's EBU R128 meter). The first play test's recording of the snow stage sat at -7.6 LUFS with peaks over full scale, the engine's 66 Hz on top, where the other games in the same recording sat near -25; the bed (engine, wind, rain, other trucks) came down 9 dB and the one-shots 5 dB, so on that recording's scale it is about -16 LUFS. That cut overshot: on the Master bus, measured with dot-audio's `addons/dot_audio/tools/loudness.sh` over `tools/loudness.tscn`, Snowline in snow and wind with two stand-ins read -35.1 LUFS, 18 units under the family's -17 (dot-audio's `docs/loudness.md`). Every constant came up 17 dB together (rain 15, since it measured 1 LU over the band) and the stage now reads -17.7 LUFS in snow and wind and -15.9 in rain, true peak under -4 dBTP, with dot-audio's master limiter as the backstop. Under the weather each route has a stock bed (`DdAudio.ROUTE_BED`: wind in the mountains, birds and a breeze in the valleys, the same at the lot). Other trucks share one level between them (`others_heard`) and are each detuned a little, so a convoy summed in phase is not one engine 6 dB louder.

**An engine is one loop whose pitch follows the speed and whose load follows the throttle**, so each loop is changed in place with `DotAudioManager.shape(handle, volume_scale, pitch_scale)` (dot-audio API level 2), in the same units `play()` was given; restarting a loop to change its pitch is a click a frame. `DdAudio._shape` eases each loop's level towards its target, stops it below -60 dB (the pool gets the voice back) and starts it again whenever it should be heard and is not, which also covers another truck's engine refused at its start for being beyond its 60 m and a quiet loop whose voice was given to something more important. Another truck is fed by `feed_other` every frame it is drawn; one not fed is gone and its loop stops.

**Everybody on foot steps** through dot-audio's `DotAudioFootsteps` (in `DdAudio`, `keep_walkers` once a frame from `DdClient` with this player's walker and every other walker drawn, stand-ins too), a step per stride walked, so nothing about gait is sent. The ground says what it is made of with a `surface` meta: the lot's slab `concrete`, the land's roads `asphalt` (walked as concrete: the stock has no asphalt), the land itself `grass`, which is also the default. `headless_run` checks the three by asking a `DotAudioFootsteps` itself (`surface_under`). **On foot there is no engine under the mix**: a walk round the lot measured -27.9 LUFS with the steps at the stock level, so out of a truck the bed is raised by `FOOT_BED_DB` (11 dB, eased over a second and back at the wheel) and every step is `STEP_DB` (8 dB) over the stock: -17.0 and -16.9 LUFS on two walks (dot-audio's `loudness.sh` with its own walking stand-in, the game's default scene), while the snow drive stayed at -17.8.

**Rock coming down within 120 m is felt as well as heard**: a dot-fx `rock` shake with distance falloff (`ROCK_SHAKE_FALLOFF`, 70 m), added to the camera after it follows the truck and taken off before the next follow, so the jolt never feeds into the chase camera's easing. The settings' shake slider reaches it through `bind_fx`. The weather's rain and snow are particles round the camera, a state rather than an effect, and stay the client's.

## The cameras stay out of the mountain, and the server may hold one

The chase and high cameras ease toward a place behind and above the truck (`_chase_at`) and are then swept from `PIVOT_HEIGHT` (3.2 m) over the truck by a dot-core `DotCameraProbe` against `DdRoute.CAMERA_LAYER` (layer 20). Eased with nothing between, the chase camera sat in the cliff on the inside of a hairpin and in a checkpoint's banner (the first play test, 2:12 and 2:46). The mountain collides with nothing a truck can touch, so it gets a camera-only trimesh of the drawn mesh (`_build_camera_mountain`), the gates get camera-only boxes, and the road and debris carry the layer as well as layer 1; trucks are on layer 1 alone, so another truck never stops the camera. A truck that moved more than `CUT_DISTANCE` (20 m) in a frame was teleported (a respawn, a restart, a route start), and the camera cuts to it instead of easing across the map through the rock (the play test's fly-through at 1:27).

The camera key (F5, the family's view key) cycles chase, cab and high under the server's `mp_forceview`, the family's cvar bound here because this game links no controller: 0 any, 1 the cab only (the first-person view), 2 chase and high only. REPLICATED, and followed through the link (`_follow_view_rule`). `tools/camera_check.sh` drives the client's own camera down every road of every mountain at 30 m/s and fails on a frame inside, armed by the eased place being in or behind the rock on some frames; it checks `mp_forceview` too, and `--render` draws each route's worst frame before and after, and the cab.

## The light is the family's daylight; the colours stay the mountain's

The family's look pass (game-arena's built-in maps: pastel prototype grid, daylight dot-lighting) was judged against this game and only its light was taken. **The palette was not**: the world is rock, grass, asphalt, snow and water, and the weather a route is built around is read from those colours (a white road is snow, a dark glossy one is wet, a grey one is grip). A pastel grid over a mountain would make every surface a block-out and take the snow's meaning away.

- **The light is a dot-lighting document** (`DdClient._build_environment`): a warm sun with soft shadows at the old angle, a pale cool fill, filmic tone map, glow, with this game's own sky colours painted after the rig.
- **The weather still drives it**: the sun dims to half its clear value under rain or snow (`_clear_sun`), and the fog is exponential, thickened per sky (`FOG_CLEAR`, `FOG_RAIN`, `FOG_SNOW`: 0.0006, 0.0015, 0.0028), so the document carries none. Half what it was: at 0.006 the snow stage was white on white past 60 m in the first play test, and the next bend was not there to see.
- **The surfaces are photographed** (`assets/polyhaven/`, CC0, greyed and levelled so the zone's colour still says what the weather did): layered cliff rock on the cliffs, the drops, the debris and the mountain's grain, cracked asphalt on the road, loose stone on gravel. Triplanar, so nothing needs a UV.

## Signs read from the driving side, and the pointer is out of the way

A Label3D faces its +Z; in the road's frame (forward is -Z) that is back toward a truck coming up it. The checkpoint arches had one label turned to -Z and double-sided, so from the driving side every number and DEPOT read backwards (the first play test). Every label is one-sided now, and a gate has one on each face (`Label`, `LabelBack`); `handling` checks every gate and board of two routes. The mouse pointer is the client's (`DdClient.pointer_mode`, every frame; dot-menu's `manage_pointer` is off here): captured on a desktop (hidden in a browser, where capture needs a click) while driving, there for the lot, the menu, the console and a chat line. On foot in a browser the frame never asks for capture (`_apply_pointer`): the browser refused that every frame, thousands of console errors while loading, and then rate-limited even the click; the left click takes it (`_capture_on_click`).
- **Compatibility** (this game's and the shell's renderer) clips at white, so `light_profile` is dot-lighting's `painted()` (API 3), which matches the light to the renderer, lowers the exposure, grades and adds a shadowless fill (turned with the sun in `_build_environment`); at the old hand-set sun a snowy road clipped to flat white and lost its edge. Glow starts above white so a snow road does not bloom. Shadows stay on in the browser. Check both renderers (`GODOT=<a wrapper adding --rendering-method forward_plus> tools/shot.sh`).

## Project settings

`project.godot` carries no comments: the editor drops them, and any key left at its default, the first time it saves the file, so the reasons for its settings live here. Gravity stays the engine default and nothing reads it: `DdConfig.gravity` is what the world is tuned against and `DdGame` writes it onto its own physics space, because a project setting does not travel inside a delivered pack. Physics runs at the default 60 ticks on purpose: a `VehicleBody3D`'s raycast suspension is stable at 60 and visibly bouncy at 30. The renderer is `gl_compatibility`, what the delivered client runs. Texture imports keep `detect_3d/compress_to=0` (`[importer_defaults]` starts new ones that way) so the editor never re-imports one VRAM-compressed on a first open.

## Notes moved from code comments

**One world holds every route at once, and trucks are not predicted** (`DdGame`): dot-vehicle's decision, for its reason: two machines diverge on a rigid body within seconds, and a correction on something being steered reads worse than latency. What every machine computes for itself is what is a pure function: the road (from the document) and the weather (from the seed and the tick). A person arrives on foot in the lot (`DdLot`), where the trucks are on show and sold; walking is the owner's machine's (it reports where it got to, and `DdGame.walk` refuses a step too long or a place nobody on foot may be), and a driver is a record on the server rather than a body: what others see of somebody on foot is `Driver.walker`, a node the bridge replicates. Stand-ins never walk the network.

**The bridge sends little because nothing is predicted** (`DdNetBridge`): no input buffer, no reconciliation, no replay. The roads are sent again only when the world is rebuilt on another mountain (MOUNTAIN, the same field HELLO carries, applied through `adopt_documents`).

**The mouse pointer is the client's, every frame**, not dot-menu's (`manage_pointer` is off): the menu captured the pointer when it closed even with the lot up, and nothing hid it on the road. A browser grants pointer lock only inside a click, and asking every frame without one was refused thousands of times and then rate-limited.

**The chase camera is swept, not set**: eased toward a point behind the truck with nothing between, it sat in the rock on a hairpin and in a checkpoint's banner. dot-core's `DotCameraProbe` sweeps a sphere from over the cab.

**Light**: `painted()` is the family's profile for a world lit by the rig alone. The Compatibility renderer has no headroom above white, and at Forward+'s numbers a snowy road clipped to flat white, so it lowers the exposure and adds a shadowless fill; glow starts above white or a snow road blooms into the sky.

**Audio levels** were set on the Master bus with dot-audio's `loudness.sh` over `tools/loudness.tscn`: the engine bed is quiet because a bed that never stops at effect level sat at -7.6 LUFS, and the one-shots stand out of it. Each truck's engine is a little sharp or flat of the next, because the same loop summed in phase was one engine 6 dB louder.

**Terrain**: every vertex within `BAND` metres of a road edge is held below that sample's slab, so the hillside never rises through the lane; heights use `DdWeather.unit` of the route id, not `hash`. The grid margin is 50 m short of a neighbour's road: at 80 m the last 35 m had to drop sixty metres and every route stood in a lake. A cliff tapers inside its wall's stretch (eroded, then smoothed): dilated, the mountain began before its wall with no collision under it. Where two legs of a switchback meet, the ground blends with the nearest other road by inverse distance cubed.

**Suspension and grip** (`DdTrucks.tunables_for`): a load sits over the back axle, so the more of the weight is load the less the front holds; rear grip above 1 keeps a truck from spinning at full lock. Stiffness 75 is as far as it goes (110 crawled the Semi's trailer test and hopped off two wheels); relaxation damping 1.25 removes the hop and 1.5 slowed the Semi.

**Chat lines and voice** travel on this game's own wire: `DotChatRouter` has the rules, and a second path through the server's chat manager would be a second set of rules. `DotVoiceRouter.relay` stamps the transport's peer id over the packet's own speaker field, or any client could speak as any other.
